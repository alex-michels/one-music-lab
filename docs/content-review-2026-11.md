# Learning material: accuracy review, 2026-11-14

This is the evidence record for a second pass over the shipped learning
material, requested by the owner: the text should be accurate, consistent,
professionally worded and lead a learner through each topic. It continues the
[editorial review of 2026-09-10](learning-editorial-review.md). Review and
implementation: Claude, AI-assisted. No external subject or language approval is
claimed, and none is required for the owner's personal learning scope.

[ROADMAP](../ROADMAP.md) remains the only completion register. Nothing here
closes a roadmap item, completes gate G for a topic or authorizes publication.

## What was read

All 19 lessons with their formulas, reading supplements and experiment
instructions; all 141 glossary entries; the figure descriptions; prompts,
options and feedback of every exercise generator and practice rule; the
unscored notation tasks; the Notes-lab examples; the Chords-lab templates and
their notes; the starting check; and the German interface catalogue. Text was
read in English, Russian and German against the code that generates or draws
what it describes.

## Method, and what it is not

1. Twelve reviewers, one per group of files, listed every statement they judged
   wrong, misleading, inconsistent between the three languages, or untrue of
   the figure or generated item it describes: 240 findings.
2. Each finding was then checked by a second, independent reviewer whose task
   was to refute it against the repository's own sources, the code and the
   reference it cited. 201 were confirmed, 37 refuted and 2 left undecided.
   Refuted and undecided findings changed nothing.
3. Confirmed corrections were applied by file group. In ten of the twelve
   groups the applied changes were then read by a reviewer who had not written
   them, against the finding and the generator or figure concerned; their
   corrections are part of the result. The changes of the *chords-library* and
   *exercise-generators* groups (`lib/chords.ts`, `lib/exercises.ts` and their
   tests) were checked by their tests only: a second read was started and did
   not complete.
4. The integrating edits that followed (cross-file corrections the group
   agents could not make, the Russian term for equal temperament, record
   versions, these documents) had a final read-only review with three lenses
   (behaviour and tests, content, truth of the documents), each finding
   challenged by a separate reviewer. Its 26 confirmed findings were fixed
   before this record was written; 3 were refuted.
5. The Russian changes to lesson and glossary text were read in full by the
   integrating reviewer, and every changed value of the public views was listed
   from a comparison of the last commit with the working tree (see
   *Fingerprints* below).

The *Basis* column in the tables is the reference recorded with the finding.
Only 139 of the 201 confirmed findings record one; several are general
references, and a number of them say themselves that a page or edition is still
to be checked, that they were cited from memory or that they were seen only in
a search summary. **These citations have not been re-read by the owner. They
are not claim-level source verification.** The tables abridge *What was wrong*
to 200 and *Basis* to 180 characters (marked "…"); the full records stayed in
the review's working files and are not part of the repository. Every lesson
source record keeps the status it had (`inherited-unverified`).

Where a correction would have added a statement that the lesson's source does
not carry, it was held back (`sound-all-1`, `tuning-all-3`), with one
exception: `timbre-all-1` now states how the harmonics of the ideal square,
triangle and sawtooth waves fall off (1/k, 1/k², 1/k). These laws were checked
on 2026-11-14 against Wolfram MathWorld, *Fourier Series—Square Wave* (eq. 7),
*—Triangle Wave* (eq. 7) and *—Sawtooth Wave* (eq. 10), but the timbre lesson's
own source record does not carry them yet.

Among the changes of behaviour, not only of wording, each with tests: a natural
note is asked for through its neighbour instead of being printed in its own
question; tuplet questions offer the unscaled written value and no value longer
than the bar; the level-one excerpt puts the right note at any of four places;
the metronome explanation counts one click in the singular; ornament, beaming
and dynamics rubrics describe the one figure on screen; a sign read from
another octave has its own feedback (`carried-the-sign-to-another-octave`); the
repeat question no longer names the bars it asks about; the double sharp, the
duplet and the cross-bar tie are engraved as their text says; a moved chord's
Russian label reads «на 1 октаву вверх».

Terminology made consistent across the three languages: Russian
«равномерная темперация» throughout (the glossary headword was «Равномерный
строй», which stays as a search alias); «ключ соль / фа / до»; «основная
ступень»; the tie and the slur named apart, the tie as «связующая лига» in its
lesson and glossary headword (the Notes lab, the tasks and the articulation
lesson say «лига продления», which the dots-and-ties lesson equates with it
and which is now a search alias of that entry) against «артикуляционная лига»;
German *Haltebogen* and *übergebunden* for the tie, *Versetzungszeichen*
against *Vorzeichnung*, and the *du* form throughout.

Smaller alignments made while integrating: the English body of *Reference
pitch* now names the lab's note as Russian and German did; Russian and German
say "modern" notation where English did for the scope of an accidental; German
writes "1200 Cent" throughout; the repeats formula writes "al Fine" as its text
does.

## Guided walk-throughs

Every lesson gained a `guide`: the question it answers, one example in three to
five steps, what to listen for, one common mix-up and three or four things to
keep. The rule is in [content-schema.md](content-schema.md#the-guided-walk-through):
a guide may only restate or apply what its own lesson already says, or do plain
arithmetic on its numbers. Each guide was written from its lesson and then read
against it by a second reviewer for added facts; what that reviewer removed or
reworded is in the result. Tests check the shape, the conventions of each
language and that a quoted example name is the label of a button beside it;
whether a guide adds no fact is this editorial check, not a test, and not a
subject expert's approval.
